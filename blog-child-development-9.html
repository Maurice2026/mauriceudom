Parenting Styles and Approaches in Early Childhood

By Maurice Udom | 12 min read

Every parent has a style. Some are strict enforcers of rules. Others are easygoing and permissive. Some balance warmth with structure. Your parenting style—the characteristic ways you interact with, guide, and discipline your children—profoundly influences their development.

Understanding different parenting approaches helps you identify your style and reflect on whether it's serving your family well.

Classic Parenting Styles

Authoritative Parenting

High warmth + High structure. Authoritative parents set clear limits and expectations while being responsive and loving. Research consistently shows this style produces the best developmental outcomes: children who are confident, competent, and emotionally healthy.

Authoritarian Parenting

Low warmth + High structure. Rules are rigid; obedience is demanded. While some structure is good, the lack of warmth can affect emotional development and parent-child relationships.

Permissive Parenting

High warmth + Low structure. These parents are loving but provide minimal boundaries. Children may struggle with self-regulation and limits.

Uninvolved Parenting

Low warmth + Low structure. Children lack both connection and guidance. This style is associated with poorest outcomes for development.

Most effective: Authoritative parenting that combines love with appropriate boundaries.

Modern Parenting Approaches

Attachment Parenting

Emphasizes secure attachment through responsive parenting, co-sleeping, breastfeeding. Focus is on following the child's cues and needs.

Gentle/Respectful Parenting

Combines structure with respect for the child's perspective. Focuses on connection and understanding rather than punishment.

Positive Discipline

Uses natural consequences and problem-solving rather than punishment. Focuses on teaching, not controlling.

Montessori Parenting

Follows the child's interests, minimizes adult control, provides appropriate structure and environment.

Finding Your Style

Your parenting style is influenced by how you were parented, your values, personality, and circumstances. Awareness is the first step toward intentional parenting—choosing how you want to parent rather than defaulting to patterns.

The goal isn't perfection. It's consistency, warmth, and appropriate guidance. It's being present, responsive, and willing to adjust as your child grows.
