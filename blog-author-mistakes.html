7 Mistakes First-Time Authors Make When Publishing a Book

Hard-won lessons from decades of writing, publishing, and editing — so you can avoid the traps that derail most debut authors.

Publishing your first book is exciting and terrifying. The temptation to rush it into the world is overwhelming. But haste, inexperience, and misplaced priorities destroy more debut books than lack of talent ever will.

After thirty years of writing, publishing, and editing the manuscripts of other authors, I have seen the same seven mistakes repeated by first-time authors around the world.

The Seven Critical Mistakes

1. Publishing Before the Book Is Ready — Your first draft is not a finished book. The real writing happens in revision. Skipping revision means publishing a rough, unfinished product.

2. Skipping Professional Editing — You cannot objectively evaluate your own work. A professional editor sees what is actually on the page and catches errors you will never notice.

3. Using an Amateur Cover — A professional, genre-appropriate cover is essential. An amateur cover signals that the content inside is equally amateur.

4. Having No Marketing Plan — If you do not tell people your book exists, they will not find it. Marketing does not have to be expensive, but it must be intentional.

5. Pricing the Book Wrong — Research competitive pricing in your genre. For ebooks, $2.99–$9.99 is standard. For print, $9.99–$19.99 depending on length.

6. Ignoring Formatting — Poor formatting makes your book look unprofessional and is distracting for readers. Use dedicated formatting tools.

7. Not Building an Author Platform — Your book is one product. An author platform — website, blog, email list, social media — is a long-term asset.
