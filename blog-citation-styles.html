APA vs MLA vs Chicago: Which Citation Style Should You Use?

A quick, clear guide to the three most common citation styles — so you never format a reference incorrectly again.

Citation styles exist for one reason: to give credit to the sources you use in a consistent, verifiable way. But with multiple styles in use across different disciplines, choosing the right one — and formatting it correctly — can be confusing.

APA Style (7th Edition)

Best for: Psychology, education, social sciences, nursing, business. APA emphasises the date of publication.

In-text citation format: (Author's Last Name, Year). For direct quotes: (Author, Year, p. 45).

MLA Style (9th Edition)

Best for: Literature, languages, cultural studies, philosophy, religion, and the arts. MLA focuses on authorship and the text itself.

In-text citation format: (Author's Last Name Page Number) — no comma, no "p."

Chicago Style (17th Edition)

Best for: History, some humanities, and publishing. Chicago offers two systems: Notes-Bibliography and Author-Date.

Notes-Bibliography — Footnote format: A superscript number in the text links to a footnote.

How to Choose

In most cases, you do not choose — your institution tells you which style to use. Always check the assignment guidelines before you begin writing. The most important thing is consistency throughout your entire paper.
