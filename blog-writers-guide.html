Why Every Writer Should Read African Fiction

By Maurice Udom  |  6 min read

As writers, we are always searching for new voices, new perspectives, and new ways of telling the human story. If you have not yet explored African fiction, you are missing one of the richest and most powerful literary traditions in the world.

Writers grow by reading widely. African fiction offers something truly unique: stories rooted in ancient oral traditions, rich cultural complexity, and raw human truth.

Africa — a great storytelling civilisation

Africa and other ancient civilisations share profound storytelling traditions. Both have produced literature that grapples with identity, family, and belonging. Today's African writers are reshaping global literature on their own terms, writing with full confidence in their own cultures.

"The best literature shows you something true about your own world. African fiction does exactly that for every reader, everywhere."

What African fiction teaches writers

Reading African fiction will make you a better writer. African storytelling understands that a story is not just events — it is a living thing, connected to community, ancestors, and the land. This produces fiction with depth and resonance.

  • How to weave oral storytelling techniques into written fiction
  • How to give voice to community and collective experience
  • How to handle themes of identity with power and grace
  • How to write characters who carry the weight of their history
  • How to find the universal in the deeply specific and local

Nigerian fiction — a world of its own

Nigeria has produced an extraordinary literary tradition. With over 500 languages and a storytelling tradition stretching back thousands of years, Nigerian fiction offers unlimited creative material. Nigerian stories deal with power, loyalty, love, and the supernatural — rooted in unique cultural reality.
