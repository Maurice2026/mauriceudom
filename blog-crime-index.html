About This Series

When a severe crime commands public attention, the instinct is to reach for a single, simple explanation — poverty, mental illness, moral failure, or pure evil. The scientific consensus tells a fundamentally different story. Serious violent crime — homicide, murder-suicide, arson resulting in death — is never the product of one cause acting alone. It arises at the intersection of biological vulnerabilities, psychological conditions, sociological pressures, and situational triggers that converge in ways that are identifiable, analysable, and in many cases preventable.

This series applies the biopsychosocial framework — the standard analytical model used by forensic psychologists, criminologists, and behavioural scientists worldwide — to trace the full arc of severe violent crime. It begins with why crimes happen, moves through the human and social consequences of extreme violence, and concludes with the forensic, digital, psychological, and institutional mechanisms through which perpetrators are eventually brought to account. The series is written as peer-reviewed research and is intended for scholars, practitioners, policymakers, students, and informed general readers.

Each article stands independently and may be read in any order. Read as a complete series, they constitute a comprehensive, evidence-based account of one of the most consequential questions in the human sciences: why do people commit extreme violence, and what follows when they do?

The Biopsychosocial Framework at a Glance

What the series establishes
Biological

Prefrontal cortex deficits, amygdala dysregulation, neurochemical imbalance, and traumatic brain injury lower the threshold at which aggression becomes possible.

Psychological

Untreated psychosis, personality disorders, and maladaptive coping shape the form and direction of aggression and suppress the constraints that ordinarily prevent harm.

Sociological

Structural strain, domestic coercion, adverse childhood experiences, and social learning in violent environments activate and amplify biological and psychological vulnerability.

Situational

Substance intoxication, acute crisis, perceived humiliation, and weapon access provide the final ignition that transforms accumulated vulnerability into destructive act.

Consequences

The act imposes total, propagating consequences on perpetrator, victim, families, and community — consequences that compound across generations without effective intervention.

Justice

Forensic science, DNA technology, digital surveillance, witness disclosure, and behavioural self-betrayal ensure that permanent escape from accountability is, in the overwhelming majority of cases, an illusion.
