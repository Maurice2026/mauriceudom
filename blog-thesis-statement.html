How to Write a Thesis Statement

The Heart of Your Paper

Academic Writing · Essay Structure · 9 min read

A thesis statement is a single sentence that states the main argument of your essay. It is clear, specific, and arguable — supported by the rest of your paper.

Without a strong thesis, your essay drifts. Every paragraph, sentence, and piece of evidence should support your thesis. It is the foundation.

What Makes a Thesis Strong?

Specific: Not "Technology is important." Instead: "Artificial intelligence will change human work by 2035."

Arguable: Not "Shakespeare wrote plays." Instead: "Shakespeare's portrayal of women reflects his era's patriarchal anxieties."

Focused: One main idea. Your thesis guides the essay.

Thesis vs Topic Sentence

A thesis statement appears in your introduction and states the main argument of the entire paper. A topic sentence introduces the main idea of a body paragraph. Your thesis is the umbrella; your topic sentences are the sections under it.

Weak ThesisSocial media has changed how people communicate.
Strong ThesisWhile social media enables global communication, it erodes interpersonal relationship quality by encouraging shallow, performative interactions.

How to Develop Your Thesis

Ask yourself: What is the one claim I want my reader to believe? Your answer is your thesis seed. Refine it. Make it specific, arguable, and focused.

Your thesis may change as you write. Many writers discover their true thesis halfway through. This is normal. Thesis statements are flexible until your essay is finished.
