Self-Publishing in Nigeria: A Practical Complete Guide

By Maurice Udom  |  13 min read

Everything you need to know about publishing your own book — from Nigeria or anywhere else. Platforms, pricing, marketing strategies, and the realistic timeline for turning your manuscript into a published book that earns money. Based on seven years and seven published works.

The Self-Publishing Revolution

Ten years ago, self-publishing was considered the last resort for writers who could not get a "real" book deal. Today, many of the highest-earning authors are self-published. The barriers to entry have fallen — you no longer need permission from a traditional publisher to reach readers. All you need is a finished manuscript, basic design skills (or money to hire a designer), and understanding of how to upload your book to publishing platforms.

But self-publishing is not just hitting "publish" and hoping for sales. It requires the same professionalism as traditional publishing — editing, cover design, formatting, and marketing. The difference is that you control everything. The tradeoff is that you are responsible for everything.

Step 1: Finish and Polish Your Manuscript

Before you even think about publishing, your manuscript must be complete and professionally edited. This is non-negotiable. Readers can forgive plot weaknesses if the writing is brilliant. They cannot forgive typos and grammatical errors. Invest in professional editing — it is the single best investment you can make in your book's success.

What "finished" means:

  • First draft complete (3-6 months for most writers)
  • Self-revision (2-4 weeks)
  • Beta reader feedback (2-4 weeks)
  • Major revisions based on feedback (2-6 weeks)
  • Professional developmental editing (4-8 weeks)
  • Professional copy editing (2-4 weeks)
  • Final proofreading (1-2 weeks)

Step 2: Design Your Cover

Your cover is your book's first and often only chance to capture a reader's attention. A bad cover kills sales, no matter how good the content. A great cover sells books even before the first page is read. Either hire a professional cover designer or use a platform like Canva Pro to create something compelling. Avoid generic templates — your book deserves to stand out.

Step 3: Format for Print and Digital

Formatting is different for print and digital books. Print (paperback) requires specific margins, fonts, and layout. Digital (e-book) requires a different structure for readability on small screens. Each platform has requirements. It is worth paying for professional formatting or using templates designed specifically for your chosen platform.

Step 4: Choose Your Publishing Platforms

In Nigeria and globally, your main options are:

Amazon KDP (Kindle Direct Publishing)

The largest ebook and paperback platform in the world. Low royalty rates (35-70% for ebook depending on pricing), but massive audience. Global reach. Takes 24-48 hours to go live.

Selar.com (Nigeria-focused)

Nigerian platform perfect for African authors. Higher royalty rates (60-90%). Growing audience. Payment to Nigerian accounts. Start here if you are in Nigeria.

Smashwords

Distributes to multiple platforms (Apple Books, Kobo, etc.) from one upload. Good for wide distribution. Lower royalty rates.

IngramSpark

For professional print books. Higher setup costs, but your book appears in bookstores and libraries worldwide. Worth it for literary fiction and academic books.

Step 5: Pricing Your Book

Price too high, and you will not sell. Price too low, and readers will assume low quality. Most self-published fiction prices between $2.99-$4.99 (ebook) and $9.99-$14.99 (paperback). Non-fiction and academic work prices higher: $4.99-$7.99 (ebook), $15-$25 (print). Research comparable books in your genre to find the sweet spot.

Step 6: Marketing and Launch

Publishing is not the end — it is the beginning of work. You must market your book. Traditional publishers spend significant resources on marketing their books; as a self-published author, that responsibility falls on you. Your options include: email marketing to your list, social media promotion, paid advertising on Amazon and Facebook, reaching out to book bloggers and reviewers, and building author platforms on Goodreads and other reader communities.

"Publishing your book is not the finish line — it is the starting line." — Chris Guillebeau

The Real Timeline and Cost

Realistically, the self-publishing process takes 6-12 months from a finished manuscript to your book being available for sale. Costs range from $500 (if you do most of the work yourself) to $3,000+ (if you hire professionals for cover design, editing, and formatting). This is an investment in your career. The books you publish will continue earning royalties for years.

After your first book, the process speeds up. You know what works. You have contacts with editors and designers. Your second book will be faster and easier.
