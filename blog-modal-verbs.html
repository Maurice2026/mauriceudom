Master English Modal Verbs: Complete Usage Guide

Grammar · English Language · 11 min read

Modal verbs confuse many English learners. Master these nine verbs, and your English becomes more nuanced and sophisticated.

Modal verbs express meaning beyond simple action. They show ability, permission, obligation, possibility, and probability. Understanding when to use can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, and would is crucial for fluent English.

What Are Modal Verbs?

Modal verbs are auxiliary verbs that express mood or attitude toward an action. They modify the meaning of main verbs. The nine modal verbs in English are: can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, and would.

CAN vs COULD

CAN: Expresses present ability, permission, or possibility. "I can swim." "Can I leave early?" "It can happen."

COULD: Expresses past ability, polite requests, or remote possibility. "She could run fast when young." "Could you help me?" "It could rain tomorrow."

MAY vs MIGHT

MAY: Expresses permission or probability. "You may go." "It may rain." (more formal than "might")

MIGHT: Expresses possibility or permission (less formal). "It might rain." "Might I ask a question?"

MUST vs SHOULD vs SHALL

MUST: Expresses obligation or strong necessity. "You must attend." "There must be a mistake."

SHOULD: Expresses recommendation, expectation, or mild obligation. "You should sleep more." "It should arrive today."

SHALL: Used in formal contexts for offers or future. "Shall I open the door?" (primarily British English)

WILL vs WOULD

WILL: Expresses future, willingness, or habitual action. "I will attend." "Will you help?" "She will always arrive late."

WOULD: Expresses conditionality, habit (past), or polite request. "I would go if invited." "He would always call." "Would you mind?"
