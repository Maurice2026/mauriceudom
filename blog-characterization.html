Characterisation as an Asset to Novel Writing

Master the craft of creating compelling, memorable, and believable characters that drive your story.

Great novels are built on great characters. Plot may capture attention for a chapter or two, but character keeps readers turning pages until midnight. A reader may forget the exact events of a story, but they will never forget a character who felt real.

Characterisation is not decoration in a novel. It is the engine. When you get characterisation right, readers invest emotionally in your characters' fates. When you get it wrong, even the most exciting plot falls flat.

The Elements of Strong Characterisation

Strong characters have depth. They have contradictions. They want something, they are afraid of something, and they have a reason to do what they do. They are not cardboard cutouts spouting dialogue.

Physical appearance matters less than many writers believe. What matters is how the character thinks, how they speak, what they fear, what they desire, and how they change.

Building Authentic Characters

The best characters are built from contradiction. A tough detective who secretly writes poetry. A confident CEO who is insecure about her marriage. A selfish man who makes one selfless sacrifice. Contradiction makes characters feel human.

Give your characters clear motivations. Why do they do what they do? A character who acts without clear motivation is not mysterious—they are incomprehensible.

Character Development and Arc

Your protagonist should change from the beginning of the novel to the end. This change is the character arc. A character who remains exactly the same has no arc—and without an arc, the reader may wonder why they were reading about this person at all.
