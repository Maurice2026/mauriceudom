Academic Integrity & Plagiarism Policy

Effective: May 2026

⚠ Be careful — plagiarism is not allowed and will result in an automatic zero.
Ad 728×90 leaderboard

1. Definition of Plagiarism

Plagiarism is defined as presenting someone else's work, ideas, or language as your own without proper acknowledgment. This includes:

  • Direct Copying: Copying word-for-word from a classmate, a book, or an online source.
  • Paraphrasing without Citation: Rephrasing an idea without crediting the original author.
  • Collusion: Submitting collaborative work as your individual effort.
  • Self-Plagiarism: Submitting the same work across multiple assignments.

2. Detection Methods

All assignments are subject to verification using:

  • Digital Comparison Tools: Cross-referenced against previous submissions and internet resources.
  • Peer-to-Peer Checking: Compared against other students in the current session.
  • AI Metadata Analysis: Document version history and metadata are monitored.
Ad 336×280 rectangle

3. Consequences of Academic Dishonesty

Students found to have plagiarized will face:

  1. An immediate score of zero for the assignment.
  2. A formal report submitted to the department head.
  3. Requirement to resubmit with a maximum possible grade of Pass only.

4. How to Avoid Plagiarism

Visit our Linguistics Blog and the Lecture Series in the sidebar for guides on citing sources and summarising academic monographs.
