Section 1 Introduction to Report Writing

Purpose and Importance

Reports document findings, present data, make recommendations, and propose actions (Ober, 2008). In organizational settings, reports drive decision-making. A well-written report can secure funding, approval, or implementation of important projects.

Definition: A report is a structured document that presents information, data, findings, and recommendations to a specific audience for a specific purpose (Munter & Hamilton, 2014).

Report Writing vs. Other Writing

Unlike essays or articles, reports must be objective, data-driven, and clearly organized (Bovée & Thill, 2005). Readers expect specific information in predictable locations. Personal opinion is appropriate only in recommendation sections clearly labeled as such.

Section 2 Types of Reports

Informational Reports

Informational reports present facts and findings without recommendations (St. Onge, 2018). Examples include status reports, incident reports, and research reports. The writer's role is to inform, not persuade.

Analytical Reports

Analytical reports examine problems, evaluate options, and often include recommendations (Munter & Hamilton, 2014). Feasibility studies and comparative analysis reports are analytical. These reports require careful reasoning and evidence.

Sample Analytical Report: The Decline of Small-Scale Retail Businesses in Urban Areas

Prepared by: Chidinma Nwankwo | Date: 24th May, 2026

This report analyses the decline of small-scale retail businesses in urban areas of Nigeria. Small retail shops such as provision stores, electronics shops, clothing boutiques, and other family-owned businesses have been an important part of Nigerian urban commerce for many decades. However, in recent years, many of these small businesses have closed down, and the number of new small retail businesses opening has decreased significantly. This report examines the reasons why small retail businesses are declining and considers what options business owners have to survive in the current commercial environment. The analysis is based on observation of trends in urban areas, interviews with small business owners, and information from business publications and reports.

Small retail businesses have traditionally been important sources of employment and income in Nigerian cities. These businesses employed family members and hired workers, contributing to local economies and providing goods and services to communities. In the past, small retail shops were very common on every street in residential and commercial areas. Families would inherit these businesses from parents and continue operating them. The business model was simple but effective: buy goods in bulk, sell them in smaller quantities to customers at a profit, and use the money to reinvest in the business and support family needs. This system worked well for many years and was how many families built wealth and stability.

Several important factors are causing small retail businesses to decline. The first major factor is the growth of large supermarkets and shopping malls. These large retailers have advantages that small shops cannot match. They can buy goods in much larger quantities at lower prices, allowing them to offer lower prices to customers. They have modern facilities with air conditioning, wider selections of products, and convenient parking. Many customers prefer shopping at these large stores because they can find everything they need in one place. Small retailers cannot compete with these low prices because they buy in smaller quantities and pay more per unit. As a result, customers are choosing large retailers over small shops, and small retailers are losing business.

The second factor is the rise of online shopping and e-commerce platforms. Many people now shop online using their phones and computers, ordering goods to be delivered to their homes. E-commerce companies like Amazon, Jumia, Konga, and other platforms offer convenience, wide product selection, and sometimes lower prices. Young people especially prefer shopping online rather than going to physical shops. This has reduced the number of customers visiting small retail shops. Small business owners say that they cannot compete with online retailers because they do not have the technology infrastructure or financial resources to establish online shopping platforms.

The third factor is the high cost of operating a retail business. Small shop owners must pay rent for their shop space, which is very expensive in urban areas. They must also pay for electricity, water, security, and other utilities. When business is slow and sales are low, these fixed costs continue even though the owner is making little profit. Many small shop owners say that their rent and operating costs are so high that they cannot make enough profit to justify keeping the business open. Additionally, accessing credit or loans to expand business or purchase inventory is very difficult because banks require collateral and proof of income that small businesses cannot easily provide.

Given these challenges, small retail business owners have several options to consider. The first option is to accept that the traditional retail model is not viable and to close the business and seek employment elsewhere. This is unfortunate but realistic for many owners who cannot compete with large retailers and e-commerce platforms. The second option is to specialise in niche products that large retailers do not sell. Instead of trying to sell general goods like large supermarkets do, small retailers could focus on specialised products such as traditional local foods, handicrafts, customised clothing, or speciality items that large retailers do not stock. The third option is to combine physical retail with online sales. Some small business owners are starting to use social media platforms like Instagram, Facebook, and WhatsApp to take customer orders and deliver goods to homes. The fourth option is to focus on excellent customer service and build customer loyalty. While large retailers offer low prices, small retailers can offer personal service, understanding of customer needs, and loyalty programmes.

In conclusion, small-scale retail businesses in urban areas face serious challenges from large supermarkets, online shopping platforms, and high operating costs. Traditional retail models are no longer as viable as they were in the past. However, small retailers are not without options. By specialising in niche products, combining physical retail with online sales, providing excellent customer service, and modernising their shops, small retailers can survive and continue to serve important roles in their communities. The future of small retail businesses depends on owners being willing to adapt, learn new methods, and understand changing customer preferences. Those small retailers who are able to innovate and differentiate themselves from large competitors will be able to survive, while those who continue with traditional methods without any changes will likely close down.

Proposal Reports

Proposals persuade readers to approve or fund a project or action (Ober, 2008). Successful proposals clearly articulate problems, propose solutions, and justify why approval is warranted. They are sales documents disguised as objective reports.

Section 3 Report Structure and Format

Standard Report Components

Formal reports typically include: title page, executive summary, table of contents, introduction, body sections, conclusions, recommendations, and references (Turabian, 2013). Each section serves a specific purpose and contains specific information types.

Key Components:
• Title Page: Title, author, date, recipient
• Executive Summary: Brief overview of entire report
• Table of Contents: Numbered sections and page numbers
• Introduction: Background, purpose, scope
• Body: Detailed findings and information
• Conclusion: Summary of main points
• Recommendations: Actions proposed based on findings
• References: Sources cited

Section 4 Executive Summaries and Abstracts

Executive Summaries

Executive summaries allow busy decision-makers to understand reports without reading everything (Bovée & Thill, 2005). They should include purpose, main findings, and key recommendations in 1-2 pages. Summaries should be self-contained and understandable by themselves.

Abstracts

Abstracts are brief summaries of research reports or studies, typically 100-150 words (Turabian, 2013). Unlike executive summaries for decision-makers, abstracts help researchers decide whether to read the full report.

Section 5 Data Presentation and Visuals

Using Charts and Graphs

Visuals communicate complex data more effectively than text alone (Munter & Hamilton, 2014). Choose appropriate visual types: bar charts for comparisons, line graphs for trends, pie charts for proportions. Each visual should have a clear title and legend.

Tables and Data Organization

Tables present detailed data that would be difficult to explain in text (St. Onge, 2018). Organize rows and columns logically, label clearly, and reference visuals in text before they appear.

Section 6 Proposal Writing

Proposal Elements

Effective proposals include clear problem statements, detailed solutions, justification for why the solution will work, and specific budgets and timelines (Ober, 2008). They must convince readers that the proposed action is necessary and appropriate.

Persuasive Proposal Strategies

Proposals should emphasize benefits to the reader, address potential objections, and provide evidence that similar projects have succeeded (Bovée & Thill, 2005). Credibility is essential; unsupported claims undermine proposals.

Sample Proposal: Establishment of a Community Skills Training Centre in Lekki District

Submitted by: Adaobi Okoro, Community Development Officer | Date: 24th May, 2026

Lekki District is experiencing a serious problem of youth unemployment and lack of marketable skills among young people. Many young people between the ages of eighteen and thirty-five are without jobs because they do not have the skills that employers are looking for. The secondary schools in the area teach mainly academic subjects but do not provide practical training in trades and skills that young people can use to earn money. After finishing secondary school, many young people either sit at home doing nothing or engage in unproductive activities. Girls especially have limited opportunities because there are few places where they can learn income-generating skills such as sewing, hairdressing, or food processing. Young men are unable to learn trades such as carpentry, plumbing, electrical work, or welding. This lack of skills means that many young people in Lekki District remain unemployed and cannot contribute productively to their families or the community. This proposal seeks to address this problem by establishing a community skills training centre where young people can learn practical skills that will help them find employment or start their own businesses.

To address the youth unemployment problem, we propose to establish a Community Skills Training Centre that will offer short-term practical training courses in various trades and skills. The centre will be located in a suitable building in the Lekki community where there is enough space for classrooms, workshops, and practical training areas. The centre will be operated by trained instructors who have practical experience in the trades they teach. The centre will offer courses in the following areas: for young men, there will be courses in carpentry, plumbing, electrical installation, welding, and motor vehicle repair; for young women, there will be courses in tailoring and fashion design, hairdressing and beauty care, catering and food processing, and soap and cosmetics production. Each course will last three months and will combine classroom learning with practical hands-on training. Upon completion of the course, students will receive a certificate that shows what skills they have learned.

This proposal will bring many benefits to Lekki District. First, it will reduce youth unemployment by providing young people with the skills that employers want. Young people who complete the training will be able to find jobs in their chosen trades, which will improve their economic situation and that of their families. Second, the training will enable young people to start small businesses as self-employed artisans and traders. Third, the centre will contribute to the economic development of Lekki District by increasing the productive capacity of the population. When young people have skills and income, they spend money in the community, which helps other businesses and creates a cycle of economic development. Fourth, reducing unemployment will reduce crime and social problems that result from idleness and poverty. Young people who have jobs and income are less likely to engage in criminal activities. Fifth, the centre will help young women become independent and economically self-sufficient, which improves their status in society.

The centre will be established in phases. In the first phase, which will take three months, a suitable building will be found and renovated to create classrooms and workshops. Qualified instructors will be recruited and trained. Equipment and tools needed for the various trades will be purchased. In the second phase, which will take one month, the centre will be officially opened and the first students will be recruited. In the third phase, which is the ongoing operation phase, the centre will conduct courses regularly. Each course will last three months. As soon as one group of students completes a course, a new group will start. The centre will aim to train at least fifty students every three months, which means two hundred per year.

The total cost to establish and operate the Community Skills Training Centre for one year is estimated as approximately one million two hundred and eighty thousand Naira. Building renovation and setup will cost two hundred thousand Naira. Equipment and tools for the workshops will cost three hundred and fifty thousand Naira. Instructors' salaries for one year will cost five hundred thousand Naira. Learning materials, office supplies, and miscellaneous expenses will cost one hundred and fifty thousand Naira. Utilities such as electricity and water will cost eighty thousand Naira per year. In subsequent years, the main expenses will be salaries for instructors and utilities, which will be approximately six hundred and thirty thousand Naira per year. This cost will be covered by the training fees collected from students and by seeking financial support from government agencies and development organisations.

Youth unemployment in Lekki District is a serious problem that requires urgent action. The establishment of a Community Skills Training Centre is a practical and effective solution that will help young people acquire the skills they need for employment and self-employment. The centre will contribute to reducing poverty and creating economic development in the community. The total investment required is reasonable and the potential benefits are substantial. Therefore, we strongly recommend that the Local Government Development Authority approve this proposal and provide the necessary financial and administrative support to establish the Community Skills Training Centre. The centre will make a real difference in the lives of young people in Lekki District and will help build a more prosperous community for everyone.

Section 7 Recommendation Reports

Structure and Purpose

Recommendation reports analyze options and recommend one course of action (Munter & Hamilton, 2014). They present evidence for and against each option, then explicitly recommend the best choice with reasoning.

Making Persuasive Recommendations

Recommendations must be based on clear criteria and weighted analysis (Turabian, 2013). Simply stating a preference is not persuasive; recommendations require evidence that the recommended option best meets stated criteria.

Section 8 Feasibility and Progress Reports

Feasibility Reports

Feasibility reports evaluate whether projects can succeed given constraints of budget, time, and resources (St. Onge, 2018). They typically conclude that projects are feasible, feasible with modifications, or not feasible. Clear criteria help readers understand the basis for conclusions.

Progress Reports

Progress reports update stakeholders on project status: work completed, current work, and work remaining (Ober, 2008). They address whether projects are on schedule and on budget. Bad news should not be hidden; transparency builds trust.

Section 9 Technical Documentation

Purpose and Characteristics

Technical documentation explains complex systems, procedures, or products to users or technicians (Bovée & Thill, 2005). It must be accurate, complete, and organized in logical sequences. Users should be able to follow instructions without additional help.

User Manuals and Instructions

Effective instructions use numbered steps, clear language, visuals where helpful, and warnings where necessary (Munter & Hamilton, 2014). Testers should try to use instructions without assistance; unclear instructions require revision.

Section 10 Evaluating Report Quality

Report Evaluation Criteria

Effective reports are accurate, clear, complete, well-organized, and appropriate for their intended audience (Turabian, 2013). They use visuals appropriately, support conclusions with evidence, and follow formatting conventions consistently.

Reader-Focused Revision

Quality reports address reader questions directly and provide needed information in accessible locations (St. Onge, 2018). Revision should focus on clarity from the reader's perspective, not just from the writer's perspective.
