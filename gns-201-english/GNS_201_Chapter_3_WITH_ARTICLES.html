Section 1 Introduction to Writing for Publication

Purpose and Audience

Writing for publication differs fundamentally from academic writing or personal writing. Published writing must engage a specific audience, maintain reader interest, and deliver clear value (Murray, 2014). Understanding your audience is the first step in publication writing.

Definition: Writing for publication is writing created for public consumption, including essays, articles, blog posts, and opinion pieces intended to inform, entertain, or persuade readers (King, 2000).

Characteristics of Published Writing

Effective published writing is clear, engaging, and purposeful (Trimble, 2000). It must grab attention, maintain interest, and deliver on its promise. Structure is invisible but essential; readers should follow your ideas easily without noticing the scaffolding.

Section 2 Essay Writing and Structure

The Essay Form

Essays are organized arguments on a specific topic. Academic essays, personal essays, and editorial essays share common structural elements: introduction, body development, and conclusion (Turabian, 2013). However, each type adapts this structure for its purpose and audience.

Essay Introductions

Effective introductions capture attention, establish context, and preview the essay's main argument (Murray, 2014). They should begin with something specific and compelling—an anecdote, surprising statistic, or provocative question—before narrowing to the thesis.

Body Development

Body paragraphs develop the thesis with evidence, examples, and analysis (King, 2000). Each paragraph should have a clear topic sentence that relates to the thesis. Transition between paragraphs should be smooth, showing how each idea builds on previous ones.

Essay Conclusions

Conclusions should synthesize ideas and provide closure rather than simply restating the thesis (Trimble, 2000). Strong conclusions often end with broader implications, unresolved questions, or calls to action that leave readers thinking.

Section 3 Thesis Development and Argumentation

Crafting Strong Theses

A thesis statement is the main argument the essay proves or explores (Booth et al., 2011). Strong theses are specific, arguable, and clear. Weak theses are too general, obvious, or make unsupported claims.

Thesis Examples:
❌ Weak: "Social media is important."
✓ Strong: "Social media platforms should implement stricter regulations on misinformation to protect public discourse."

Building Arguments

Arguments require evidence, reasoning, and acknowledgment of opposing views (Toulmin et al., 1979). The strongest arguments anticipate counterarguments and address them respectfully, demonstrating critical thinking rather than simple assertion.

Sample Article 1: The Erosion Problem in Okuju Community

By Chioma Adeyemi | 24th May, 2026

Okuju is a small farming community in Akwa Ibom State in southeastern Nigeria. For many years, this community was known for producing healthy crops and feeding many families. However, today the situation has changed completely. The community is now facing a serious problem of soil erosion that is destroying the farmland and making life very difficult for the people who live there. When you visit Okuju now, you will see large gullies that have appeared in the ground, some of them very deep and wide. These gullies have destroyed farmland that families have used for generations, and the problem keeps getting worse every year. This article discusses the serious erosion problem in Okuju and what it means for the people living there.

The erosion in Okuju is very severe and affects almost every part of the community. From what people in the community say, nearly half of the land that was good for farming has been damaged or completely destroyed by the erosion. There are several large gullies that have grown very big, some reaching more than twenty metres deep and thirty metres wide. The biggest gully, which the people call "The Great Scar," stretches almost two kilometres through the community and has destroyed everything in its path. What makes this problem even worse is that the gullies are still growing every time it rains, eating away more and more land. Families who have used the same farmland for many years are now watching it disappear right before their eyes.

One farmer named Ikechukwu Etim shared his sad story about what the erosion has done to his family. He is now sixty-three years old and his father gave him about twenty hectares of good farmland when he was young. But now, because of the erosion, he only has three hectares left, and even that land is in danger from the advancing gullies. He said with deep sadness that he does not know how his children and grandchildren will survive when the land that his family has depended on for so long is gone. His story is similar to the stories of many other farmers in Okuju who are losing their land to erosion. These farmers feel helpless because there is nothing they can do to stop the gullies from getting bigger and bigger.

There are several reasons why the erosion problem in Okuju has become so serious. The main reason is that many trees have been cut down in the area. In the past, the forests covered the hills and protected the soil, but now most of the trees are gone because of logging and the need to clear land for farming. Without the trees and plants to hold the soil in place, the rain now washes away the soil easily and creates the gullies. Another problem is that there is no good drainage system to manage the heavy rains that fall during the rainy season. When heavy rain comes, the water flows down the hills without any control and cuts deeper and deeper channels that become the gullies. Also, farmers have been farming on the steep hillsides without protecting the soil with any conservation methods, which makes the erosion even worse. Climate change is also playing a role because the rains seem to be heavier and more destructive than before.

The erosion in Okuju is causing very serious problems for the people living there. Most families in the community depend on farming to get food and money to survive. Now that the farmland is destroyed, families do not have enough food to eat and they cannot earn money from selling crops. This has created a food security problem, especially for children who are not eating enough nutritious food. Health workers in the community have noticed that more children are becoming malnourished, particularly when stored food runs out before the next harvest. Because farming is no longer possible on much of the land, many young people have left the community to find work in the cities. This is causing the community to become weaker because the young people who should be helping their families and developing the community are leaving. Schools are losing students because families are moving away, and there are fewer children to teach. The economy of the community is collapsing because there is no money coming in from farming anymore. Many families are very poor now and struggling to buy even basic things they need.

The government response to the erosion problem in Okuju has been very disappointing. Although government officials sometimes visit the community and say they are concerned about the problem, they have not actually done very much to help. No major government project has been started to control the erosion. A few organisations and NGOs have tried to help with small projects, but these are very small compared to how big the problem actually is. The community is begging for help, but help has not come. The people of Okuju feel abandoned by the government and they are losing hope that the situation will improve.

To stop the erosion problem in Okuju, several things need to be done. First, the government must build proper drainage systems to manage rainwater and prevent it from creating more gullies. Second, the hills need to be replanted with trees and plants to hold the soil in place and stop erosion. Third, farmers need to learn and practise better farming methods that protect the soil, such as planting crops on contours and not on steep slopes. Fourth, the government must give money to repair the damage that has already been done by building barriers and other structures to stop the gullies from growing. All of these solutions require money and careful planning, but if they are not done soon, the situation will only get worse. The community needs help from the federal government, the state government, and international organisations that work on environmental problems.

In conclusion, the erosion problem in Okuju is a serious crisis that must be addressed urgently. The community has lost much of its farmland and the people are suffering from food shortages and poverty. Young people are leaving the community because there is no future for them there. The government has not provided adequate help to solve the problem. Without immediate and serious action to control the erosion and rehabilitate the damaged land, the situation will continue to get worse and the Okuju community may not survive as a viable farming community. The people of Okuju deserve government support and investment in their land and their future. Action must come now before it is too late and the damage becomes permanent and irreversible.

Sample Article 2: The Crisis of Poor Health Facilities in Nigeria

By Segun Oladele | 24th May, 2026

Last year, a woman named Adenike went to the local hospital because she was having severe chest pains and could not breathe well. She walked over four kilometres with her husband helping her to reach Lekki District Hospital, hoping the doctors would help her. When they arrived, she found that there was no doctor on duty that night. The equipment for checking her heart was broken and had not been repaired for many months. She waited in a dirty waiting room with many other sick people for six hours, getting weaker and weaker. Unfortunately, before any doctor could see her, she collapsed and died from a heart attack. What happened to Adenike is very sad, but sadly it is not uncommon in Nigeria. Many people die from health problems that should not be deadly because our hospitals are not working properly. This article discusses the serious problem of poor health facilities in Nigeria and what it means for the health of our people.

The problem with health facilities in Nigeria is very widespread and very serious. Many hospitals and health centres across the country do not have enough doctors, nurses, and medicines. Equipment that is needed for diagnosis and treatment is broken or does not exist. Many health centres do not have clean water or proper toilets. Some hospitals do not have reliable electricity, which means that important medical equipment cannot work. The buildings themselves are in very poor condition, with broken roofs, crumbling walls, and dirty floors. In many areas, especially rural areas, there are no health facilities at all or the facilities that exist are so bad that people prefer to go to traditional healers or prayer houses instead of going to hospitals. This situation means that preventable diseases kill people because they cannot get proper medical care. Pregnant women die during childbirth from complications that could be treated if the hospital had the right equipment and skilled doctors. Children die from malaria and other diseases that are easily cured if treated properly.

One major problem is that there are not enough doctors and nurses in our hospitals. Many healthcare workers have left Nigeria and gone to work in other countries like Britain, America, and the Middle East because the pay is much better and the working conditions are good. A doctor I know had to study for many years to become a doctor so that he could help sick people. But after working in Nigeria for a few years and earning very little money while struggling with broken equipment and no medicines, he decided to leave and now he works in London earning much more money and working in a proper hospital. This doctor told me that he is sad to have left his country but he cannot feed his family or live with dignity on what the government pays him. His story shows why many healthcare workers are leaving Nigeria. When doctors and nurses leave, the hospitals have empty positions that are not filled. This means the remaining doctors and nurses have to work very hard, looking after too many patients, and they become tired and frustrated.

The lack of medicines in hospitals is another serious problem. The government does not have enough money to buy medicines, so many pharmacies in hospitals do not have the drugs that patients need. If a patient comes to hospital with an infection, the doctor may prescribe antibiotics, but the pharmacy does not have those antibiotics. The family must then go to private pharmacies and buy expensive medicines, which many poor families cannot afford. This means sick people either buy cheap fake medicines from the street, or they do not take any medicine at all and their illness gets worse. Diabetics cannot get the insulin they need to control their blood sugar. People with high blood pressure cannot get blood pressure medicines, so they have strokes. Cancer patients cannot complete their chemotherapy because the drugs run out. All these deaths could be prevented if the government could provide the medicines that hospitals need.

The poor conditions in hospitals are also a problem. Many hospitals do not have clean water for washing hands or cleaning wounds. Some hospitals do not have functioning toilets, which spreads disease. Operating theatres that should be very clean are dirty and have old equipment. Some hospitals do not have electricity at night, so if a patient needs emergency surgery at night, there is no light in the operating theatre. Laboratory equipment for testing blood and diagnosing diseases is broken or absent, so doctors must diagnose patients without proper testing. All of these problems mean that patients who go to hospital for treatment sometimes catch new infections while they are in the hospital, or their conditions get worse instead of better.

Because the government health system is not working well, people are suffering from many preventable deaths. Diseases that were almost controlled, like tuberculosis, are coming back because patients stop taking medicine when it is not available, creating drug-resistant strains. Malaria is still a big killer because although we know how to prevent and treat it, many people do not have access to prevention and treatment. Pregnant women are dying from complications that could be prevented or treated with skilled birth attendants and proper equipment. Babies are dying from easily preventable causes because they cannot access basic healthcare. These are not deaths that should happen in the modern world. These deaths happen because our health system has failed.

The reason for this health system crisis is that the government has not given enough money to health for many years. The government says it will spend six percent of its budget on health, but it actually spends much less, maybe three or four percent. This means there is not enough money to build new hospitals, fix broken ones, buy medicines, or pay doctors and nurses good salaries. When money is very low, the government sometimes cuts health budgets even more to pay for other things. Also, sometimes government officials steal health money for themselves, so even the little money that is available does not reach the hospitals. There is not good management of the health system, and equipment is sometimes bought that is not needed while important equipment is not available.

To improve health in Nigeria, the government must do several important things. First, it must spend more money on health, meeting the promise of six percent of the budget. This money must be used to repair hospitals, build new health centres, and buy medicines and equipment. Second, the government must pay doctors and nurses better salaries so they will stay in Nigeria instead of going to other countries. It must also provide opportunities for them to continue learning and developing their skills. Third, the government must stop corruption so that the money that is given to health actually reaches the hospitals and is used for patients. Fourth, the government must ensure that all health facilities have basic things like clean water, electricity, and proper sanitation systems. Finally, medicines must be supplied regularly to all hospitals so that sick people can get the treatment they need.

In conclusion, the health facilities in Nigeria are in a serious state of crisis. Many people are dying from diseases that should not be deadly because they cannot get proper medical care. Doctors and nurses are leaving the country because they are not paid well and conditions are poor. Medicines are not available in hospitals. Equipment is broken and not repaired. The buildings are falling down. These problems are not new, but they have been getting worse for many years because the government has not given health the importance it deserves. If the government does not act urgently to fix the health system, many more preventable deaths will happen. Nigeria has the resources to build a good health system that can care for its people, but it must choose to make health a priority and invest the necessary money and effort. The people of Nigeria deserve a health system that can help them when they are sick. Without urgent action, many Adenikes will continue to die while waiting in broken hospitals.

Section 4 Supporting Evidence and Research

Types of Evidence

Effective writing uses multiple types of evidence: facts and statistics, examples and case studies, expert testimony, and logical reasoning (Murray, 2014). Evidence must be relevant, current, and from credible sources.

Research Ethics

All sources must be properly attributed. Plagiarism—using others' words or ideas without attribution—is both unethical and harmful to your credibility (Turabian, 2013). Even paraphrasing requires citation.

Section 5 Article Writing and Journalism

News Articles

News articles present information in order of importance, with essential details in the first paragraph and supporting details following (Harrower, 2012). This "inverted pyramid" structure allows readers to understand the story even if they don't read the entire article.

Writing Headlines

Headlines must be accurate, specific, and engaging while fitting spatial constraints (Keeble, 2005). They should summarize the story's most important element and entice readers to continue.

Section 6 Feature Writing and Creative Nonfiction

Feature Stories

Features go beyond breaking news to explore topics in depth, often focusing on human interest angles (Harrower, 2012). They use narrative techniques, dialogue, and vivid description to engage readers emotionally while conveying information.

Creative Nonfiction

Creative nonfiction uses literary techniques (narrative structure, dialogue, sensory details) while maintaining factual accuracy (Miller & Spellmeyer, 2006). The goal is truth told artfully rather than artistry masking truth.

Section 7 Opinion Pieces and Editorial Writing

Op-Ed Writing

Op-eds are argumentative pieces that express author opinions on current issues (Keeble, 2005). Unlike news articles, they advocate for a position. They should be timely, relevant, and supported by evidence even though they express opinion.

Logical Fallacies to Avoid

Opinion writing must avoid common logical errors: ad hominem attacks (attacking the person rather than the argument), straw man arguments (misrepresenting opposing positions), and circular reasoning (Booth et al., 2011). Strong opinion writing defeats opposing arguments with evidence, not dismissal.

Section 8 Editing and Revision Techniques

The Revision Process

Professional writers revise extensively, often more than they initially write (King, 2000). Revision is not proofreading; it involves reconsidering argument, rearranging ideas, and cutting unnecessary material.

Self-Editing Strategies

Effective self-editing involves reading aloud to catch awkward phrasing, checking that each paragraph has a clear purpose, and ensuring transitions connect ideas logically (Murray, 2014). Getting feedback from others reveals what readers actually understand versus what you intended.

Section 9 Publishing and Submission Process

Choosing Publication Venues

Research potential publications thoroughly before submitting (Trimble, 2000). Understand their audience, typical article length, writing style, and submission process. Submitting appropriate work to appropriate venues dramatically increases acceptance chances.

Query Letters and Pitches

Query letters pitch article ideas to editors before writing (Murray, 2014). A strong query is brief (usually one page), demonstrates why the topic is timely, and shows why you're qualified to write it.

Section 10 Citation and Credibility

Citation Styles

Different disciplines use different citation formats: MLA for humanities, APA for social sciences, Chicago for history and some humanities (Turabian, 2013). Know which style your publication uses and apply it consistently.

Building Credibility

Credibility comes from accurate information, proper citation, acknowledging limitations, and representing opposing views fairly (Booth et al., 2011). Readers trust writers who are transparent about their sources and honest about what they don't know.
