10 Must-Read Nigerian Plays Every Literature Student Should Know

A curated reading list of the plays that defined Nigerian drama — from Nobel laureates to the bold new voices shaping the future of African theatre.

Nigerian drama is one of the richest theatrical traditions in the world. From Wole Soyinka's mythological landscapes to the sharp social satire of younger playwrights, Nigerian plays have confronted power, corruption, gender, tradition, and the complexities of post-colonial identity with a force and originality that demands global attention.

01
Death and the King's Horseman
Wole Soyinka (1975)

Based on a true historical event in colonial Oyo, this play explores the clash between Yoruba metaphysical belief and British colonial authority. This is arguably the finest play ever written by an African. It earned Soyinka the Nobel Prize for Literature in 1986.

TragedyYoruba CultureColonialism
02
The Lion and the Jewel
Wole Soyinka (1963)

A charming comedy set in the village of Ilujinle, where a schoolteacher and a traditional chief compete for the affections of the village beauty, Sidi. Beneath its humour lies a sharp critique of the tension between modernity and tradition.

ComedyTradition vs Modernity
03
The Gods Are Not to Blame
Ola Rotimi (1968)

Rotimi's masterful adaptation of Sophocles' Oedipus Rex transplants the Greek tragedy into a Yoruba setting, proving that the themes of fate, pride, and self-destruction are universal.

TragedyFateAdaptation
04
The Marriage of Anansewa
Efua Sutherland (1975)

Using the Ananse storytelling tradition, Sutherland creates a witty, participatory play about a father who auctions his daughter's hand in marriage. A masterclass in using traditional performance forms within modern theatre.

ComedyStorytelling
05
Our Husband Has Gone Mad Again
Ola Rotimi (1977)

A rollicking comedy about a retired military officer turned politician whose three wives create chaos when they discover each other. Rotimi uses the domestic farce to satirise political ambition and class pretension.

ComedyPolitical Satire
06
Wedlock of the Gods
Zulu Sofola (1972)

One of the earliest published plays by a Nigerian woman, Wedlock of the Gods tells the story of a young widow forced by tradition to marry her late husband's brother. Sofola's unflinching examination opened doors for future women playwrights.

TragedyGenderTradition
07
The Blind Gods
Maurice Udom

A searing tragedy that exposes the deep-rooted injustice against women in patriarchal societies. The play examines how tradition, power, and silence conspire to destroy the lives of women who dare to demand justice.

TragedyGender JusticePower
08
Morountodun
Femi Osofisan (1982)

Osofisan reimagines the Yoruba heroine Moremi in the context of modern class struggle and worker exploitation. A powerful critique of class, privilege, and the moral cost of complicity.

Political DramaClassRevisionist
09
The Golden Home
Maurice Udom

A biting comedy of greed and self-delusion. Aladima, a university graduate reduced to working as a gatekeeper, blackmails his employers. The play holds a devastating mirror to corruption and ambition.

SatireComedyCorruption
10
Emotan
Ola Rotimi (2003)

Rotimi's final major work dramatises the legend of Emotan, the Benin market woman whose bravery helped restore a rightful king to the throne. The play celebrates female heroism and political courage.

Historical DramaFemale HeroismBenin
