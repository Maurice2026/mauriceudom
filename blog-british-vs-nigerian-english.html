The Difference Between British English and Nigerian English

Key differences in vocabulary, grammar, idioms and pronunciation — and when each variety is appropriate in academic writing.

English is the world's lingua franca, but it is not uniform. British English, American English, Nigerian English, Indian English, Australian English — each variety has distinct vocabulary, grammar patterns, and idioms shaped by history, culture, and education.

If you are a Nigerian student writing essays for international audiences, or a professional communicating across borders, understanding the differences between British and Nigerian English is essential. Using the wrong variety at the wrong time can undermine your credibility and confuse your readers.

Vocabulary Differences

British: Flat, petrol, post box, queue, aeroplane, holiday, mobile phone, colour.

Nigerian: Apartment, fuel, post office, line, airplane, leave, cell phone, color.

Notice that Nigerian English incorporates American influences alongside British foundations. This reflects Nigeria's exposure to both varieties through education, media, and technology.

Grammar and Articles

Nigerian English sometimes omits articles in ways British English would not. For example, a Nigerian speaker might say "I went to market" (British: "I went to the market"). Similarly, Nigerian English may use present continuous where British English uses simple present: "What are you doing?" (Nigerian) vs "What do you do?" (British, asking about your occupation).

Verb Forms

British: "I have got a pen" or "I've got a pen."
Nigerian: "I have a pen" (more direct, following American influence).

British English uses the present perfect extensively; Nigerian English sometimes prefers simpler structures.

Pronunciation and Stress

British: Stress on first syllable — AD-dress, CON-test, RE-search.
Nigerian: Often stress final syllable — ad-DRESS, con-TEST, re-SEARCH.

This difference matters less in writing but affects how your work is received when read aloud.

Which Variety Should You Use?

For academic writing, you should choose one variety and use it consistently. Most Nigerian universities accept either British or Nigerian English, but consistency is non-negotiable. Mixing varieties looks careless and undermines your argument.

If writing for an international audience, British English has broader recognition. If writing for a Nigerian or West African audience, Nigerian English demonstrates cultural awareness and authenticity.
