About Lecture Series 6: Organisational Communication

This lecture blog series is designed for students in tertiary institutions studying Organisational Communication. Each chapter is presented as a self-contained lecture that can be read independently or followed sequentially as a complete course, covering every major topic from foundational communication theory to practical strategies for improving communication effectiveness in real Nigerian and global organisations.

Every chapter includes detailed concept explanations with key concept boxes, real-world Nigerian workplace examples, references to contemporary academic sources (2020 onwards), structured time allocations for reading and study, and examination practice questions with mark allocations to help students prepare thoroughly for their assessments.

The series adopts a mixed citation approach — key theories and models are cited with full Harvard-format academic references, while explanations and examples are presented in clear, accessible prose suitable for tertiary-level study and professional development.

How to Use This Series

Click any chapter card below to open its dedicated lecture page. Each chapter takes approximately 65–80 minutes to study fully. Lecture Chapters

Ten fully developed lecture chapters — all available now

01
Introduction to Organisational Communication
Defining communication, the communication process model, importance of communication in organisations, the 7 Cs of effective communication, scope and dimensions, and the role of communication in organisational success and failure.
Available 70 mins 10 Questions
02
Channels of Communication in Organisations
Media richness theory, oral and written communication channels, digital and electronic channels, social media as an organisational channel (Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube, WhatsApp, TikTok, Telegram, X), channel selection criteria, and managing multi-channel communication.
Available 65 mins 10 Questions
03
Theories of Organisational Communication
Linear, interactive, and transactional models; classical, human relations, and human resources theories; systems theory; transparent communication theory; Communication Privacy Management (CPM) theory; and digital-age communication perspectives.
Available 75 mins 10 Questions
04
Vertical Communication in Organisations
Downward and upward communication — their functions, forms, barriers, and distortions; power distance and psychological safety; the mum effect and hierarchical filtering; and practical strategies for improving vertical information flow across hierarchical levels.
Available 70 mins 10 Questions
05
Horizontal and Diagonal Communication
Peer-to-peer and cross-level communication across departments — functions of horizontal communication, silo mentality and how it damages organisations, diagonal communication, matrix structures, cross-functional teams, and strategies for building a connected and collaborative organisation.
Available 70 mins 10 Questions
06
Barriers to Effective Communication
A systematic framework for diagnosing communication failure — semantic, psychological, physical, organisational, cultural, and technological barriers; why correct diagnosis matters; and targeted strategies for overcoming each barrier category in Nigerian and global organisational contexts.
Available 75 mins 10 Questions
07
Verbal Communication in Organisations
Oral and written communication — their functions, advantages, limitations, and forms; the serial transmission effect and how to prevent it; active listening as the most neglected verbal communication skill; and practical strategies for improving both oral and written communication at every level.
Available 70 mins 10 Questions
08
Non-Verbal Communication in Organisations
Kinesics, facial expressions and eye contact, proxemics and Hall's distance zones, paralanguage, appearance, chronemics, and the physical environment as communication — plus managing non-verbal communication in cross-cultural and digital/hybrid working contexts.
Available 70 mins 10 Questions
09
Formal and Informal Communication Networks
Communication network structures — chain, wheel, circle, all-channel, and Y; the grapevine — patterns, speed, accuracy, and why it matters; opinion leaders, gatekeepers, and boundary spanners; rumour and misinformation management; and integrating formal and informal networks as a strategic communication asset.
Available 70 mins 10 Questions
10
Improving Organisational Communication
The communication audit — a seven-stage diagnostic process; leadership communication as the most powerful lever; building a genuine feedback culture; communication strategy and policy; communication training and capability development; measuring effectiveness at four levels; and the ten characteristics of a truly communicative organisation — a synthesis of the entire series.
Available 80 mins 10 Questions
